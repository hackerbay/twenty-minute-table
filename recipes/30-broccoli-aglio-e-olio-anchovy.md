# 30 · Broccoli Aglio e Olio with Anchovy

**Cuisine:** Italian · **Method:** One pan · **Total time:** 15 min (5 prep / 10 cook) · **Serves:** 4

> Broccoli steamed and then browned in the same pan, dressed in anchovy-melted garlic oil and covered in toasted sourdough crumbs.

## Why it works
Anchovies dissolve completely in warm oil, leaving savoury depth and no fishiness — they season the dish the way salt does, only rounder. Steaming the broccoli in a splash of water and then letting the pan run dry means it cooks through and browns in one place, without a colander.

## Ingredients
- 2 heads broccoli (~800 g / 1¾ lb), stalks peeled and sliced, tops in small florets
- 75 ml (5 tbsp) olive oil
- 60 g (2 oz) stale sourdough, torn into rough crumbs
- 30 g (¼ cup) almonds, roughly chopped
- 8 anchovy fillets in olive oil, drained
- 6 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- ¾ tsp chilli flakes
- 1 lemon — zest and juice
- 20 g (½ cup, packed) flat-leaf parsley, chopped
- Salt and black pepper

## Method
1. Heat 2 tbsp of the oil in a large frying pan over medium. Add the crumbs and almonds and toast 2 minutes, stirring, until sandy gold and crisp. Tip into a small bowl.
2. Return the pan to low-medium heat with the remaining oil, the anchovies, garlic and chilli. Cook 2 minutes, mashing the anchovies against the base until they disappear into the oil and the garlic is pale straw.
3. Add the broccoli, 100 ml (scant ½ cup) water and a pinch of salt. Cover and cook 4 minutes over high heat, until a knife tip slides into the stalks with slight resistance.
4. Uncover and keep the heat high for 2 minutes, pressing the florets against the pan base. It is ready when the water has gone, the pan is glossy rather than wet, and the cut faces have caught brown.
5. Off the heat, add the lemon zest and juice, parsley and plenty of pepper. Scatter the crumbs over just before serving so they stay crisp.

## Chef's notes
- **Swap:** Cauliflower or romanesco on the same timings. Capers instead of anchovies for a meat-free version.
- **Make it faster:** Skip the crumbs and use toasted almonds alone — that is 3 minutes saved.
- **Bulk it out:** Toss through cooked spaghetti with a splash of its water, or serve on white beans.
- **Leftovers:** Good cold the next day. Keep the crumbs separate or they go soft.
