# 29 · Prawn Saganaki with Feta

**Cuisine:** Greek · **Method:** One pan · **Total time:** 15 min (5 prep / 10 cook) · **Serves:** 4

> Prawns cooked straight into a sharp tomato sauce, with chunks of feta dropped on at the end so they soften rather than melt away.

## Why it works
Prawns give off water as they cook, so the sauce has to be reduced properly before they go in — otherwise it thins out and slides off. Adding the feta off the heat and covering the pan warms it through while it still holds its shape and its salt.

## Ingredients
- 3 tbsp (45 ml) olive oil
- 4 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- 1 tsp dried oregano
- ½ tsp chilli flakes
- 2 tbsp (30 ml) ouzo or dry white wine (optional)
- 1 x 400 g (14 oz) tin chopped tomatoes
- 600 g (1 lb 5 oz) raw king prawns, peeled
- 200 g (7 oz) feta, in rough 2 cm chunks
- 20 g (½ cup, packed) flat-leaf parsley, chopped
- 1 lemon, in wedges
- Salt and black pepper

## Method
1. Heat the oil in a large frying pan over medium. Add the garlic, oregano and chilli and cook 1 minute, until the garlic is pale gold. Take it no further than that or it turns bitter.
2. Pour in the ouzo and let it bubble for 30 seconds, then add the tomatoes and a good pinch of salt. Simmer hard for 5 minutes, until the sauce holds a clear channel when you drag a spoon across the pan base.
3. Stir the prawns in, pushing them under the sauce. Cook 2–3 minutes, turning once, until they are pink and curled into a loose C. A tight O means they have gone too far.
4. Take the pan off the heat, scatter the feta over the top and cover for 1 minute so it warms and slumps at the edges.
5. Finish with parsley, black pepper and lemon wedges, and serve from the pan with bread for the sauce.

## Chef's notes
- **Swap:** Mussels or clams instead of prawns — cover and steam 4 minutes, discarding any that stay shut.
- **Make it faster:** Buy prawns already peeled and you lose 4 minutes of prep.
- **Bulk it out:** Stir a drained tin of butter beans into the sauce before the prawns go in.
- **Leftovers:** Keeps 2 days chilled. Reheat gently and briefly, or the prawns turn rubbery.
