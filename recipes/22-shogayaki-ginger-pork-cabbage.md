# 22 · Shogayaki — Japanese Ginger Pork with Cabbage

**Cuisine:** Japanese · **Method:** One pan · **Total time:** 12 min (5 prep / 7 cook) · **Serves:** 4

> Thin pork, a serious amount of grated ginger, and a cold mound of shredded cabbage to cut through it.

## Why it works
Ginger juice does the tenderising, so the pork needs no marinating window — it goes into the pan dry and meets the sauce only at the end. Pouring the sauce into a hot, already browned pan lets it reduce to a glaze in a minute instead of steaming the meat pale.

## Ingredients
- 600 g (1 lb 5 oz) pork loin steaks, sliced 5 mm thin
- 30 g (2 tbsp) fresh ginger, finely grated, juice and all
- 3 tbsp (45 ml) soy sauce
- 2 tbsp (30 ml) mirin
- 1 tbsp (15 ml) sake or dry sherry
- 1 tsp honey
- 1 tbsp (15 ml) rapeseed oil
- ¼ small white cabbage (about 300 g / 10 oz), shredded as finely as you can
- 1 tsp rice vinegar
- 2 spring onions, sliced
- 1 lemon, cut into wedges

## Method
1. Stir the ginger, soy, mirin, sake and honey together in a small bowl.
2. Shred the cabbage and pile it onto four plates. Sprinkle with the vinegar and a pinch of salt.
3. Heat the oil in a large frying pan over a high heat. Lay in half the pork in one layer and cook 60–90 seconds a side, until the edges are browned and no pink remains. Move to a plate and repeat.
4. Return all the pork to the pan, pour in the sauce and let it bubble hard for a minute, turning the slices, until it thickens enough to cling.
5. Slide the pork and its sauce onto the cabbage. Scatter with spring onion and serve with lemon wedges.

## Chef's notes
- **Swap:** Chicken thigh, opened out flat, takes 3 minutes a side. Thick aubergine slices work with an extra spoon of oil.
- **Make it faster:** Buy pork ready-sliced for shabu-shabu; it cooks in 40 seconds a side.
- **Bulk it out:** A bowl of rice each, and miso soup made while the pan heats.
- **Leftovers:** 2 days chilled. Chop and fold through rice with an egg for a fast fried rice.
