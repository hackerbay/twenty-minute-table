# 43 · Ras el Hanout Turkey with Herbs & Almonds

**Cuisine:** Moroccan · **Method:** One pan · **Total time:** 15 min (5 prep / 10 cook) · **Serves:** 4

> Turkey mince seared hard, spiced while it sizzles, then cooled with lemon, chickpeas and a fistful of herbs.

## Why it works
Turkey mince is lean enough to sear rather than stew, so pressing it flat and leaving it alone for two minutes builds a browned crust that stands in for a long-cooked base. The ras el hanout goes in after the browning, into hot fat, where thirty seconds is enough to bloom it without scorching the ground ginger and cinnamon in the blend.

## Ingredients
- 2 tbsp (30 ml) olive oil
- 500 g (1 lb 2 oz) turkey mince
- 1 red onion, finely sliced
- 2 garlic cloves, sliced
- 2 tsp ras el hanout
- ½ tsp fine sea salt
- 400 g (14 oz) tin chickpeas, drained
- 60 g (½ cup) whole almonds, roughly chopped
- 1 lemon — zest and juice
- 30 g (¾ cup, packed) flat-leaf parsley and coriander, roughly chopped
- 100 g (⅓ cup) thick Greek yoghurt, to serve

## Method
1. Get a large frying pan properly hot over a high heat. Add half the oil, then the mince in one layer. Press it flat and leave it undisturbed for 2 minutes — you want a brown crust before you break it up.
2. Break the mince into rough clumps, push it to one side and add the rest of the oil with the onion and garlic. Cook for 3 minutes, until the onion is slack and translucent at the edges.
3. Scatter over the ras el hanout and salt and stir everything together for 30 seconds, until the pan smells warm and resinous.
4. Add the chickpeas and almonds and cook 3 minutes more, stirring twice, until the chickpeas take colour and the almonds smell toasted.
5. Off the heat, stir through the lemon zest and juice and most of the herbs. Taste for salt — turkey needs more than you expect.
6. Spoon onto plates with the yoghurt and the remaining herbs.

## Chef's notes
- **Swap:** Chicken mince behaves identically. Lamb is richer — pour off some fat before the spices go in.
- **Make it faster:** Skip the onion and grate in a shallot; it softens in under a minute.
- **Bulk it out:** Couscous steeped in boiling water for 5 minutes, or flatbread torn into the pan.
- **Leftovers:** Three days chilled. Good cold in pitta with more yoghurt and lemon.
