# 56 · Tamagoyaki with Rice & Nori

**Cuisine:** Japanese · **Method:** One pan · **Total time:** 12 min (4 prep / 8 cook) · **Serves:** 4

> A rolled Japanese omelette built in thin layers in one pan, cut thick and laid over warm rice.

## Why it works
Cooking the egg in three thin pours rather than one thick one lets each layer set in seconds and roll while still tacky, which is what glues the spiral together. The mirin brings just enough sugar for the outside to take colour before the inside dries.

## Ingredients
- 8 eggs
- 1 tbsp (15 ml) mirin (or 2 tsp honey stirred with 1 tsp water)
- 1 tbsp (15 ml) light soy sauce or tamari, plus more to serve
- ¼ tsp fine sea salt
- 2 tsp neutral oil, for the pan
- 600 g (about 4 cups) cooked short-grain rice, warm
- 2 sheets nori, snipped into strips
- 2 spring onions, finely sliced
- 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds

## Method
1. Beat the eggs with the mirin, soy and salt for a full 30 seconds, until no ribbons of white remain.
2. Wipe a 20–24 cm (8–10 in) non-stick frying pan with an oiled scrap of kitchen paper and set it over medium heat. Keep the paper to hand.
3. Pour in a third of the egg and tilt to cover the base. When the top is barely set but still glossy, 30 to 40 seconds, roll it towards you with a spatula.
4. Push the roll to the far edge, oil the pan again and pour in another third, lifting the roll so raw egg runs underneath. Let it set, then roll it around the first. Repeat with the last third.
5. Slide the roll out, press it into a neat log and cut into 2 cm (¾ in) slices. The centre should be just set and a shade paler than the outside.
6. Divide the warm rice between four bowls, lay the slices on top and scatter with nori, spring onion and sesame. Pass more soy at the table.

## Chef's notes
- **Swap:** Grated carrot or chopped chives beaten into the eggs. Dashi in place of the water if you go the honey route.
- **Make it faster:** A square tamagoyaki pan makes a tidier roll, but a round one works and saves owning another pan.
- **Bulk it out:** Sliced avocado, quick-pickled cucumber, or a spoonful of kimchi on the side.
- **Leftovers:** Good cold in a lunchbox for 2 days; slice it thicker so it stays juicy.
